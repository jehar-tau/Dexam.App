# ADR-007 — Member ID Issuance, Activation, and Recovery

Status: Proposed — product-owner approval required
Date: 2026-09-26
Decision ID: D-007
Risk: Red — account takeover, student access, and support operations

## Decision Gate

### Decision

How should Dexam issue a student's permanent Member ID, allow the student to claim the account, and restore access when the password is forgotten?

### Option A — Email invitation and email-only recovery

Require every student to provide a unique personal email. Send activation and recovery links to that address.

- **Benefit:** Familiar implementation and simple self-service recovery.
- **Problem:** Excludes students without stable personal email, encourages use of shared guardian addresses, and contradicts the reason for approving Member ID sign-in.
- **Cost:** Email delivery eventually requires production SMTP.

### Option B — Enrolment-issued activation pack with layered recovery (recommended)

After a trusted workflow creates or matches an approved enrolment, Dexam issues one permanent Member ID and a one-time activation pack:

- a QR/link containing a high-entropy, single-use activation secret;
- a separately displayed 10-character backup activation code for manual entry;
- the permanent Member ID, shown separately from the secret;
- a 48-hour expiry and an option for authorized staff to invalidate and reissue the pack.

The activation secret is stored only as a cryptographic hash. Staff may deliver the pack but cannot set, view, or recover the student's password. Successful activation consumes every outstanding activation secret for that person.

Recovery is layered:

1. **Self-service:** send a short-lived, single-use recovery link to an already verified contact method when production delivery is configured.
2. **Assisted recovery:** an authorized employee validates the canonical person and approved enrolment evidence, then issues a one-time recovery pack. The student chooses the new password privately.
3. **Exceptional recovery:** adding or replacing the contact method during recovery requires an elevated administrator, a second staff approval, and notification to every established contact channel.

Generic responses, attempt limits, cooling-off rules, session revocation, and immutable audit events apply throughout.

- **Benefit:** Works without mandatory student email, phone, or SMS spending; staff never possess passwords; supports future self-service.
- **Problem:** Requires a carefully designed enrolment handover and an operational recovery procedure.
- **Cost:** No required provider cost for in-person/local activation; optional email or messaging delivery later.

### Option C — Staff-created temporary passwords

Staff give students a Member ID and temporary password, requiring a password change after login.

- **Benefit:** Fastest initial implementation.
- **Problem:** Staff handle reusable credentials, credentials are easily copied or sent insecurely, and accountability is weak.
- **Cost:** Low technical cost but unacceptable security and support risk.

## Recommendation

Approve Option B. It preserves the free-first model and includes students without stable personal contact details while keeping passwords private. The activation pack is a bootstrap credential, not a permanent second password.

## Member ID rules under Option B

- Format: `DXM-` followed by 12 randomly generated uppercase characters from an unambiguous alphabet that omits `I`, `L`, `O`, and `0`.
- Example: `DXM-7K3M9Q2RW5TY` (fictional).
- The ID contains no name, birth date, phone number, batch, course, location, admission year, or sequential enrolment number.
- It is generated by the trusted backend, protected by a database uniqueness constraint, immutable after issuance, case-normalized, and never recycled.
- The interface groups characters for readability but stores one canonical form.
- Knowing a Member ID is never sufficient to activate or recover an account.

## Activation controls under Option B

- Only an approved enrolment or explicitly authorized exception can issue an activation pack.
- Creating a pack does not create a second person; the workflow first runs D-004 matching and duplicate checks.
- Secrets use cryptographically secure randomness, are hashed at rest, expire after 48 hours, and are single-use.
- Resending or reissuing invalidates earlier unused secrets.
- Attempts are rate-limited by network, Member ID, and activation record without revealing which identifier exists.
- The password is entered only on the Dexam application over an approved secure connection.
- Activation creates the Supabase authentication user through a trusted server operation and links it to the existing canonical person in one recoverable workflow.
- Activation and failure telemetry never record secrets or passwords.

## Recovery controls under Option B

- Recovery screens always return the same public response and similar timing whether or not the Member ID exists.
- Recovery secrets are randomly generated, hashed, single-use, short-lived, and invalidated after success or reissue.
- A successful recovery revokes existing refresh sessions and all outstanding activation/recovery secrets.
- Standard assisted recovery may use an established verified contact plus approved enrolment evidence; it must not rely on easily discovered personal questions.
- Staff record the evidence categories checked, not copies of unnecessary personal documents or the answers themselves.
- Any recovery that replaces an established contact route is exceptional and requires two-person authorization.
- Government identity is not collected for routine recovery. Introducing it requires a separate privacy/legal Decision Gate.
- Repeated failure, suspicious contact changes, or conflicting identity evidence pauses the workflow for manual security review.

## Audit requirements

Record the actor, person, event type, time, outcome, and non-secret reason for:

- Member ID issuance;
- activation-pack creation, invalidation, expiry, and successful use;
- recovery request and approval;
- established-contact change during recovery;
- password recovery completion and session revocation;
- lock, pause, or suspected takeover escalation.

Audit records never contain activation secrets, recovery secrets, passwords, full message bodies, or unnecessary identity evidence.

## Reversibility

The expiry periods, code presentation, approved delivery routes, and recovery staffing rules can be changed later. The permanent opaque Member ID and the rule that staff cannot know passwords are intended to be durable.

## Approval requested

Approve Option B, reject it, or request changes. Approval authorizes local schema, server workflow, and automated tests. It does not authorize production messaging, collection of government identity, or a paid provider.
